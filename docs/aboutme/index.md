#About Me
Hi my name is Michael Sweeney, and I am a mechanical engineering student with an interest in understanding how individual components and systems come together to create a functional project. My interest in engineering came at a very young age through my fascination with computers, specifically how their components were assembled to work together as a complete system. Combined with my strengths and interest in mathematics, these experiences eventually led me toward mechanical engineering.

This led me to transition into Mechanical engineering. Not only was I gaining more hands-on experience I was continuing to grow my knowledge on a subject I had been very fascinated by being systems design and assembly. My education has also allowed me the opportunity to experience engineering in different environments. I began my mechanical engineering journey at the University of Tennessee before transferring to University of North Carolina at Charlotte. I also had the opportunity to study at the Universitat Politècnica de València (UPV) in Valenica, Spain, where I continued my engineering coursework in an international academic environment. This changed not only my perspectives on viewing problems but the formality and social culture of how issues should be approached depending on the culture of the people you are working with. With my education forever changing I piece together the best parts of every university to make myself the most well rounded possible. 

As I continue developing as an engineer, I am particularly interested in the intersection between mechanical engineering and artificial intelligence. I have already started to begin learning more about different AI systems and working to understand their implementation and how individual tools and processes can be integrated into larger systems. I find this process interesting for many of the same reasons that originally attracted me to computer assembly: individual components may have specific functions, but their real value comes from how effectively they are integrated into their designated system. In the future I want to develop the mechanical engineering and technical skills necessary to understand both physical systems and new innovative technologies such as Ai, with the goal of find practical real-word application where integration the 2 is required. 

### Defending an Engineering Decision:

To Me defending an engineering decision is showing why you made that decision and having confidence in your decision. I currently use equations in other engineering courses to defend my answers for different problems. I think it should be this way when it comes to engineering decisions. You should provide the evidence that supports your solution so that the reader can get a full understanding of why the decision was made. Also, it is important to use a confident tone in your decision. If you use works like I think, I just went with it, these phrases show room for negotiation and that it's not a solid decision.
